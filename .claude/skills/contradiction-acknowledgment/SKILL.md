# Contradiction Acknowledgment

Scientific literature often contains contradictory findings. Acknowledge these explicitly rather than selectively reporting or glossing over inconsistencies.

## When to Use

- Studies show opposite effects (increase vs decrease)
- Results vary by population or method
- Earlier and later studies disagree
- Effect sizes vary substantially
- Any inconsistency in the literature
- Reviewing drafts for cherry-picking

## Related Skills

- **due-diligence-research**: Find both supporting and contradicting evidence
- **citation-needed-check**: Ensure contradictory findings are properly cited
- **cross-literature-comparison**: When literatures show different patterns
- **academic-prose-writing**: Express contradictions in proper prose

## Why It Matters

**Ignoring contradictions:**
- Looks like cherry-picking
- Undermines credibility
- Misrepresents the evidence
- Can be caught by reviewers

**Acknowledging contradictions:**
- Demonstrates thoroughness
- Shows scientific honesty
- Allows nuanced interpretation
- Identifies areas needing research

## Contradiction Types

### Direction Contradictions
- Study A: Region X increased
- Study B: Region X decreased

### Presence Contradictions
- Study A: Effect found
- Study B: No effect found

### Magnitude Contradictions
- Study A: Large effect (d = 0.8)
- Study B: Small effect (d = 0.2)

### Population-Specific Contradictions
- In population A: Effect positive
- In population B: Effect negative

## Acknowledgment Framework

### Step 1: State Both Findings Clearly

**Bad (glossing over):**
> "Studies found changes in the insula following treatment."

**Good (explicit):**
> "Insula findings were inconsistent: Smallwood et al. (2016) found decreased activation, while Lee et al. (2023) found increased activation."

### Step 2: Offer Possible Explanations

**Consider:**
- Population differences
- Methodological differences
- Sample size differences
- Measurement differences
- Timing differences

**Example:**
> "These opposing findings may reflect differences in population (chronic pain vs. OCD) or task paradigm."

### Step 3: State What Can/Cannot Be Concluded

> "Given these inconsistencies, firm conclusions about insula involvement cannot be drawn."

## Language Templates

### Acknowledging Direction Contradictions
> "[Study A] found [direction 1], while [Study B] found [direction 2]. These [opposing/inconsistent] findings may reflect [possible explanation]."

### Acknowledging Null vs Positive
> "Findings were mixed: [Studies A, B] reported [effect], whereas [Studies C, D] found no significant [effect]."

### Acknowledging Evolution Over Time
> "Earlier studies [e.g., Study A] reported [finding 1], whereas later studies with larger samples [e.g., Studies B, C] found [finding 2]."

### Acknowledging Population Variation
> "Results varied by population: in [population A], [finding 1] was observed, while in [population B], [finding 2] was reported."

## Example: Full Contradiction Acknowledgment

**Topic:** Prefrontal cortex findings in smoking and schizophrenia

**Bad version:**
> "Studies found prefrontal changes in smokers with schizophrenia."

**Good version:**
> "Findings for the prefrontal cortex were mixed: Tregellas et al. (2007) reported increased gray matter in smokers, whereas later studies with larger samples found reductions (Schneider et al., 2014; Yokoyama et al., 2018). This discrepancy may reflect differences in sample size, imaging methods (VBM vs. FreeSurfer), or sample characteristics."

## Where to Acknowledge

### In Results
Report contradictions as findings:
> "The insula was identified in two studies with opposite patterns..."

### In Discussion
Interpret what contradictions mean:
> "The inconsistent findings for [region] suggest that [interpretation]..."

### In Limitations
Note impact on conclusions:
> "Contradictory findings limit the strength of conclusions about..."

## What NOT to Do

### Don't Cherry-Pick
❌ Report only studies that agree
❌ Cite only the "best" study
❌ Ignore inconvenient findings

### Don't Over-Explain Away
❌ Dismiss contradictions as "methodological noise"
❌ Claim one finding is "correct" without evidence
❌ Minimize contradictions

### Don't Leave Unresolved
❌ Note contradiction without any interpretation
❌ Leave readers confused about what to conclude

## Guidelines

- Report all relevant findings, not just consistent ones
- State contradictions explicitly, not vaguely
- Offer plausible explanations but don't overclaim
- Be honest about what cannot be concluded
- Use contradictions to identify research needs
- Let contradictions inform limitations
