# Positive Limitation Framing

Transform defensive explanations ("we excluded X because...") into purposeful methodological choices ("we focused on Y to achieve Z").

## When to Use

- User needs to explain why something was excluded
- User needs to justify a methodological limitation
- Reviewer asks "why didn't you include X?"
- Writing limitations section
- Explaining scope boundaries

## Core Principle

**Defensive (avoid):**
> "DTI was excluded because it measures white matter, not gray matter."

**Positive (preferred):**
> "We focused on gray matter outcomes to maintain tissue-type correspondence with the fMRI literature, enabling more direct structure-function comparison."

## Transformation Patterns

### Pattern 1: Exclusion → Focus
| Defensive | Positive |
|-----------|----------|
| "We excluded X" | "We focused on Y" |
| "X was not included" | "We restricted our analysis to Y" |
| "We did not examine X" | "Our scope was limited to Y" |

### Pattern 2: Limitation → Design Choice
| Defensive | Positive |
|-----------|----------|
| "A limitation is that we only..." | "Our approach was designed to..." |
| "We were unable to..." | "We prioritized..." |
| "This study lacks..." | "This study specifically examines..." |

### Pattern 3: Gap → Rationale
| Defensive | Positive |
|-----------|----------|
| "Because X doesn't exist" | "Given the current state of literature on Y" |
| "Due to lack of X" | "To align with established methods in Y" |

## Framework: FOCUS Method

**F** - Frame what you DID do (not what you didn't)
**O** - Offer rationale grounded in methodology
**C** - Connect to research question/aims
**U** - Use evidence to support the choice
**S** - State limitation honestly but briefly

### Example Application

**Issue:** Why exclude DTI studies?

**FOCUS application:**
- **F**: "We focused specifically on gray matter outcomes"
- **O**: "because the existing fMRI literature has employed standard BOLD methods optimized for gray matter"
- **C**: "Restricting our structural review to gray matter provides the most direct basis for structure-function comparison"
- **U**: "where the hemodynamic response function is well-characterized"
- **S**: "Consequently, white matter alterations were not captured in this review"

**Final version:**
> "We focused specifically on gray matter outcomes because the existing fMRI literature on psychological flexibility has employed standard BOLD acquisition and analysis methods optimized for gray matter, where the hemodynamic response function is well-characterized. Restricting our structural review to gray matter therefore provides the most direct basis for structure-function comparison with the available functional literature. Consequently, white matter alterations associated with smoking in schizophrenia, which have been reported using diffusion tensor imaging, were not captured in this review."

## Where to Apply

### Methods Section
Frame eligibility criteria as purposeful choices:
> "We focused on X to achieve Y" (not "We excluded everything except X")

### Limitations Section
Acknowledge honestly but frame as methodological choice:
> "Our focus on X was guided by Y; consequently, Z was not captured"

### Discussion
When interpreting results in light of limitations:
> "Given our focus on X, future research could extend to Y"

## Balanced Honesty

Positive framing ≠ hiding limitations. Always:
1. State what the choice was
2. Give the rationale
3. Acknowledge what was missed
4. Be honest about trade-offs

**Good balance:**
> "Although emerging evidence suggests white matter BOLD signals exist, they require specialized analytical approaches not yet applied in psychological flexibility research. Consequently, white matter alterations were not captured."

**Over-defensive (avoid):**
> "We had to exclude DTI because it's completely different and wouldn't work for our purposes."

**Deceptive (never):**
> [Simply not mentioning the limitation at all]

## Guidelines

- Always provide a rationale, not just a description
- Connect choices to research aims
- Acknowledge what was missed
- Use evidence to support methodological choices
- Be honest but not apologetic
- Frame proactively in Methods; acknowledge in Limitations
