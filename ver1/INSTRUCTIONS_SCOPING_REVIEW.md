# Scoping Review: Bridging Psychological Flexibility and Smoking in Schizophrenia

## Project Status: COMPLETE

The manuscript has been completed and is ready for submission.

---

## Overview

This scoping review follows JBI methodology and PRISMA-ScR guidelines to examine two previously unconnected neuroimaging literatures:

1. **fMRI literature on psychological flexibility** - Neural correlates of ACT interventions
2. **sMRI literature on smoking in schizophrenia** - Gray matter differences between smokers and non-smokers

**Primary Contribution**: Identifying the gap that no study has examined the relationship between brain structure and psychological flexibility.

---

## Research Questions

1. What is known about neural correlates of psychological flexibility from fMRI studies?
2. What structural brain differences exist between smokers and non-smokers with schizophrenia?
3. Has any study examined psychological flexibility in relation to brain structure?
4. What brain regions appear in both literatures, and what are the implications?

---

## Key Findings

### fMRI Literature (Psychological Flexibility)
- **4 studies** identified (N = 118 total)
- Populations: chronic pain, OCD, healthy adults (none in schizophrenia)
- Regions: ACC, insula, PCC, prefrontal cortex
- Findings inconsistent (e.g., insula ↓ in one study, ↑ in another)

### sMRI Literature (Smoking in Schizophrenia)
- **8 studies** identified (N = 64 to 1,262)
- Consistent finding: hippocampal reductions in smokers
- Also: ACC, insula reductions
- Dose-response and longitudinal evidence support causal relationship

### Critical Gap
- **No study** has measured psychological flexibility in sMRI research
- **No study** has examined brain structure in psychological flexibility research
- This gap prevents conclusions about structure-flexibility relationships
