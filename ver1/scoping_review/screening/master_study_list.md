## Study Details

### 1. Tregellas et al. (2007)
**Title**: Gray matter volume differences and the effects of smoking on gray matter in schizophrenia
**PMID**: [17890058](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17890058/)
**Sample**: 32 SCZ smokers, 32 SCZ non-smokers
**Method**: VBM (whole brain)
**Key finding**: Larger GM volume in medial frontal gyrus (↑) and STG (↑) in smokers

### 2. Van Haren et al. (2010)
**Title**: Cigarette smoking and progressive brain volume loss in schizophrenia
**PMID**: [20227855](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20227855/)
**Sample**: 159 SCZ (smoking data), 50 HC; 5-year follow-up
**Method**: Automated segmentation (whole brain)
**Key finding**: Heavy smokers showed accelerated GM loss over time in frontal, temporal, parietal regions

### 3. Schneider et al. (2014)
**Title**: Smoking status as a potential confounder in the study of brain structure in schizophrenia
**PMID**: [24373929](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24373929/)
**Sample**: 54 SCZ smokers, 135 SCZ non-smokers
**Method**: FreeSurfer (cortical thickness, subcortical volumes)
**Key finding**: Thinner DLPFC (↓), smaller hippocampus (↓ 2.2%), smaller amygdala (↓)

### 4. Jørgensen et al. (2015)
**Title**: Cigarette smoking is associated with thinner cingulate and insular cortices in patients with severe mental illness
**PMID**: [25672482](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25672482/)
**Sample**: 523 SCZ/BD smokers, 220 non-smokers
**Method**: FreeSurfer (cortical thickness)
**Key finding**: Thinner ACC (↓), thinner insula (↓), thinner lateral OFC (↓)

### 5. Yokoyama et al. (2018)
**Title**: Additive effect of cigarette smoking on gray matter abnormalities in schizophrenia
**PMC**: [PMC5890451](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5890451/)
**Sample**: 48 SCZ smokers, 52 SCZ non-smokers
**Method**: VBM (whole brain)
**Key finding**: Reduced GM in left middle frontal gyrus (↓), left precentral gyrus (↓)

### 6. Ringin et al. (2022)
**Title**: The impact of smoking status on cognition and brain morphology in schizophrenia spectrum disorders
**PMID**: [33443010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33443010/)
**Sample**: 269 SCZ smokers, 265 SCZ non-smokers
**Method**: FreeSurfer (subcortical volumes)
**Key finding**: No main effect of smoking; diagnosis × smoking interaction for thalamus and pallidum

### 7. Qiu et al. (2024)
**Title**: Associations of alcohol and tobacco use with psychotic, depressive and developmental disorders revealed via multimodal neuroimaging
**Journal**: Translational Psychiatry
**PMID**: [39112461](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39112461/)
**Sample**: Multi-site: 1,262 SCZ total
**Method**: Multimodal (VBM, CT)
**Key finding**: Reduced GM in hippocampus (↓), ACC (↓), insula (↓), temporal cortex (↓)

### 8. Musket et al. (2026)
**Title**: Hippocampal volume in schizophrenia and smoking
**Journal**: Schizophrenia Research
**Sample**: 253 SCZ smokers, 253 SCZ non-smokers
**Method**: FreeSurfer (hippocampus)
**Key finding**: Smaller hippocampal volume (↓), dose-response relationship with pack-years

## Excluded Studies (from Student's Original 13)

### DTI Only - Wrong Modality (2 studies)

| Author | Year | Title | Reason |
|--------|------|-------|--------|
| Zhang X | 2010 | Smoking and Schizophrenia Independently and Additively Reduce White Matter Integrity | DTI (FA in white matter tracts), not sMRI gray matter |
| Cullen KR | 2012 | Cigarette smoking and white matter microstructure in schizophrenia | DTI only, measures FA not gray matter volume |

**Rationale**: DTI measures white matter microstructure, not gray matter. Our theoretical framework focuses on gray matter regions. See PCC_framework.md for full rationale.

### CHR/ARMS Population - Wrong Population (3 studies)

| Author | Year | Title | Reason |
|--------|------|-------|--------|
| Welch KA | 2010 | The Impact of Substance Use on Brain Structure in People at High Risk | CHR - genetic high-risk, not diagnosed schizophrenia |
| Stone JW | 2012 | Substance Use and Regional Gray Matter Volume in Individuals at High Risk | ARMS population, not diagnosed schizophrenia |
| Koster M | 2025 | Tobacco smoking and gray matter volume in individuals at clinical high risk | CHR (NAPLS-3), not diagnosed schizophrenia |

**Rationale**: These studies examine individuals at risk for psychosis who have not yet been diagnosed with schizophrenia. Our review focuses on diagnosed schizophrenia/schizoaffective/schizophreniform disorder.

### fMRI Only - Wrong Modality (from seed literature)
- Friedman 2008, Leyba 2008, Moran 2013, Liu 2018, Liao 2019a, Liao 2019b, Yang 2019, Fan 2020, Chen 2023
- Esterlis 2014 (PET, not MRI)
