# Methods

This scoping review was conducted following the methodological framework developed by Arksey and O'Malley (2005) and refined by Levac et al. (2010) and Peters et al. (2020) for the Joanna Briggs Institute. Reporting adheres to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) guidelines (Tricco et al., 2018).

## Protocol and Registration

The scoping review protocol was developed a priori and is available from the corresponding author upon request. Scoping reviews are not eligible for registration with PROSPERO; however, the protocol follows JBI guidelines for scoping review methodology.

## Eligibility Criteria

Eligibility criteria were developed using the Population, Concept, and Context (PCC) framework recommended for scoping reviews.

### Population

Adults (≥18 years) with a diagnosis of schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder, or schizophreniform disorder based on standardized diagnostic criteria (DSM-IV, DSM-5, ICD-10, or ICD-11). Studies of clinical high-risk (CHR), ultra-high risk (UHR), or at-risk mental state (ARMS) populations were excluded, as these individuals have not yet received a formal diagnosis within the schizophrenia spectrum.

### Concept

Studies were required to address two primary concepts:

**1. Smoking status**: Studies must compare individuals who smoke with those who do not smoke. Acceptable definitions included current daily smoking, self-reported smoking status, or biochemically verified smoking. Studies that did not explicitly compare smokers versus non-smokers within the schizophrenia population were excluded.

**2. Structural brain imaging**: Studies must report T1-weighted structural MRI (sMRI) outcomes, including but not limited to: gray matter volume, cortical thickness, cortical surface area, subcortical volumes (hippocampus, amygdala, basal ganglia, thalamus), ventricular volumes, or total brain volume. Acceptable analysis approaches included voxel-based morphometry (VBM), region of interest (ROI) analysis, surface-based analysis (e.g., FreeSurfer), and automated segmentation methods.

Studies using only functional MRI (fMRI), diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), positron emission tomography (PET), single-photon emission computed tomography (SPECT), or magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS) were excluded. DTI was specifically excluded because it measures white matter microstructure (fractional anisotropy, mean diffusivity) rather than gray matter morphometry, representing a fundamentally different tissue type and analytical framework. This decision is acknowledged as a limitation.

**Multimodal studies**: Studies employing multiple imaging modalities (e.g., sMRI combined with fMRI or DTI) were included if sMRI findings were reported separately, with only the sMRI-related results extracted.

**3. Symptom severity (secondary)**: Where available, we extracted data on psychiatric symptom measures, including the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS), Brief Psychiatric Rating Scale (BPRS), Scale for Assessment of Positive Symptoms (SAPS), Scale for Assessment of Negative Symptoms (SANS), and correlations between brain structure and symptom severity.

**4. Psychological flexibility (exploratory)**: We assessed whether any included study measured psychological flexibility or related Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) constructs, including the Acceptance and Action Questionnaire-II (AAQ-II), Cognitive Fusion Questionnaire (CFQ), Comprehensive Assessment of ACT Processes (CompACT), or other validated measures of experiential avoidance, cognitive defusion, or values-based action.

### Context

We included cross-sectional, longitudinal, and case-control studies, as well as baseline data from intervention trials. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses were excluded but used for reference screening. Case reports, conference abstracts without full text, and studies with fewer than 10 participants were excluded. Only peer-reviewed publications in English with available full text were included. No date restrictions were applied.

## Information Sources

The following electronic databases were searched from inception to [search date]:

1. PubMed/MEDLINE
2. PsycINFO (via EBSCOhost)
3. Embase (via Ovid)
4. CINAHL Complete (via EBSCOhost)
5. Web of Science Core Collection
6. Cochrane Library

Additional sources included:
- Reference lists of all included studies
- Reference lists of relevant systematic reviews, including Koster et al. (2025)
- Forward citation searching using Google Scholar

A separate search was conducted specifically for studies combining psychological flexibility measures with structural neuroimaging in schizophrenia or smoking populations to document the hypothesized research gap.

## Search Strategy

The search strategy was developed in consultation with the research team and combined three concept blocks: (1) schizophrenia spectrum disorders, (2) tobacco smoking, and (3) structural brain imaging.

MeSH terms and subject headings were used where available, supplemented by free-text terms in titles and abstracts. Broad MRI terminology (e.g., "MRI," "magnetic resonance imaging," "brain MRI") was used in addition to specific structural imaging terms (e.g., "structural MRI," "sMRI," "gray matter," "VBM," "cortical thickness") to maximize sensitivity, as many studies do not specify imaging modality in titles or abstracts. Studies retrieved using general MRI terms were manually verified during screening to confirm inclusion of structural imaging data.

The full electronic search strategy for all databases is provided in Supplementary File 1.

## Selection of Sources of Evidence

Study selection was conducted in two stages:

**Stage 1: Title and abstract screening.** Two reviewers independently screened titles and abstracts against eligibility criteria. Studies clearly not meeting criteria were excluded.

**Stage 2: Full-text assessment.** Full texts of potentially eligible studies were retrieved and independently assessed by two reviewers. Disagreements were resolved through discussion and consensus; a third reviewer was available for arbitration if needed.

For this scoping review, the initial study set was derived from seed literature identified through prior work and the reference list of a recent systematic review on smoking and brain structure in clinical high-risk populations (Koster et al., 2025). This seed set was systematically screened against eligibility criteria, with formal database searches to be completed to ensure comprehensive coverage.

## Data Charting Process

A data charting form was developed iteratively based on the research questions and piloted on the first three included studies. Data were extracted independently by one reviewer and verified by a second reviewer.

## Data Items

The following data were extracted from each included study:

**Study characteristics:**
- First author, publication year, journal
- Country of origin
- Study design

**Population characteristics:**
- Sample sizes for each group (schizophrenia smokers, schizophrenia non-smokers, healthy control smokers, healthy control non-smokers, as applicable)
- Diagnostic criteria used
- Age, sex distribution
- Illness duration, age of onset
- Medication status (antipsychotic type, dose)

**Smoking variables:**
- Definition of smoking status
- Cigarettes per day
- Pack-years
- Smoking duration
- Nicotine dependence measures (e.g., Fagerström Test)
- Abstinence period before scanning

**Neuroimaging:**
- MRI field strength and scanner type
- Structural imaging method (VBM, FreeSurfer, ROI, etc.)
- Brain regions analyzed
- Statistical approach

**Outcomes:**
- Primary comparison: Smokers vs. non-smokers within schizophrenia (SZ-S vs. SZ-NS)
- Secondary: Diagnosis × Smoking interaction effects
- Tertiary: Correlations between brain structure and symptom severity
- Direction of effects (increased/decreased in smokers)
- Effect sizes where reported

**Symptom measures:**
- Scales used (PANSS, BPRS, etc.)
- Symptom scores by smoking status
- Brain-symptom correlations

**Psychological flexibility:**
- Whether any ACT-related measures were included
- Specific instruments used (if any)

## Critical Appraisal

Consistent with scoping review methodology, formal critical appraisal of included studies was not conducted, as the objective was to map the extent and nature of available evidence rather than to synthesize effect sizes or assess the quality of evidence for specific outcomes.

## Synthesis of Results

Results were synthesized narratively and organized according to:

1. **Study characteristics**: Descriptive summary of included studies
2. **Brain regions**: Findings organized by anatomical region (prefrontal cortex, hippocampus, anterior cingulate cortex, insula, temporal cortex, etc.)
3. **Comparison type**: Results stratified by:
   - Within-diagnosis comparisons (SZ-S vs. SZ-NS) [PRIMARY]
   - Diagnosis × Smoking interactions [SECONDARY]
   - Brain-symptom correlations [TERTIARY]
4. **Direction of effects**: Whether smokers showed increased or decreased gray matter relative to non-smokers
5. **Psychological flexibility gap**: Documentation of the absence of psychological flexibility measures across included studies

Tables were used to present study characteristics, extracted findings by brain region, and the pattern of results across studies.
