# Data Extraction: fMRI Studies on Psychological Flexibility/ACT

## Search Strategy

**Databases searched**: PubMed, Google Scholar
**Search terms**: (psychological flexibility OR acceptance commitment therapy OR ACT) AND (fMRI OR functional MRI OR neuroimaging OR neural correlates)
**Search date**: January 2026
**Inclusion criteria**: fMRI studies examining psychological flexibility measures (AAQ-II) or ACT intervention neural effects

## Studies Identified: 4

---

## Study 1: Smallwood et al. (2016)

| Field | Data |
|-------|------|
| **Citation** | Smallwood RF, Potter JS, Robin DA (2016). Neurophysiological mechanisms in Acceptance and Commitment Therapy in opioid-addicted patients with chronic pain. *Psychiatry Research: Neuroimaging*, 250:12-14. |
| **PMID** | 27042976 |
| **Link** | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27042976/ |
| **Population** | Opioid-addicted patients with chronic low back pain (DSM-IV opioid dependence) |
| **Sample size** | Small N (pilot study; exact N not specified in abstract) |
| **Design** | RCT: 8-week ACT vs. Health Education Control (HEC) |
| **Imaging** | fMRI (task-based, evoked pain paradigm) |
| **Psychological flexibility measure** | ACT intervention (indirect measure via treatment) |
| **Brain regions showing changes** | Decreased activation post-ACT in: MFG (middle frontal gyrus), IPL (inferior parietal lobule), insula, ACC (anterior cingulate cortex), PCC (posterior cingulate cortex), STG (superior temporal gyrus) |
| **Key finding** | First study to demonstrate neurophysiological effects of ACT in comorbid pain/addiction |
| **Limitations** | Small sample size, pilot study |

## Study 2: Aytur et al. (2021)

| Field | Data |
|-------|------|
| **Citation** | Aytur SA, Ray KL, Meier SK, Campbell J, Gendron B, Waller N, Robin DA (2021). Neural Mechanisms of Acceptance and Commitment Therapy for Chronic Pain: A Network-Based fMRI Approach. *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, 15:587018. |
| **PMID** | 33613207 |
| **Link** | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33613207/ |
| **Population** | Women with chronic musculoskeletal pain |
| **Sample size** | N = 9 |
| **Design** | Pre-post ACT intervention (4 weeks) |
| **Imaging** | Resting-state fMRI, Network-Based Statistics (NBS) |
| **Psychological flexibility measure** | AAQ-II, CPAQ (Chronic Pain Acceptance Questionnaire) |
| **Brain networks showing changes** | Reduced connectivity within and between: DMN (default mode network), SN (salience network), FPN (frontoparietal network) |
| **Key finding** | Connectivity changes correlated with AAQ-II improvement, decreased depression, reduced pain interference |
| **Limitations** | Small sample, no control group, women only |

## Study 3: Lee et al. (2023)

| Field | Data |
|-------|------|
| **Citation** | Lee SW, et al. (2023). Neural mechanisms of acceptance-commitment therapy for obsessive-compulsive disorder: a resting-state and task-based fMRI study. *Psychological Medicine*. Cambridge University Press. |
| **PMID** | 37427558 |
| **Link** | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37427558/ |
| **Population** | Patients with OCD |
| **Sample size** | N = 42 (21 ACT, 21 waitlist control) from 72 randomized |
| **Design** | RCT: 8-week group ACT vs. waitlist control |
| **Imaging** | Resting-state fMRI + task-based fMRI (thought-action fusion task) |
| **Psychological flexibility measure** | AAQ-II, AAQ for OCD |
| **Brain regions showing changes** | Task-based: Increased activation in bilateral insula, STG (superior temporal gyrus); Strengthened insula-IFG (inferior frontal gyrus) connectivity. Resting-state: PCC (posterior cingulate cortex), precuneus, lingual gyrus |
| **Key finding** | Insula-related networks may be pivotal trans-therapeutic mechanism across treatments |
| **Limitations** | OCD-specific population |

## Gap Analysis

| Combination | Studies Found | Notes |
|-------------|---------------|-------|
| ACT + fMRI + Addiction | 1 (Smallwood 2016) | Opioid/pain comorbidity |
| ACT + fMRI + Chronic pain | 2 (Smallwood 2016, Aytur 2021) | Small samples |
| ACT + fMRI + OCD | 1 (Lee 2023) | Best-powered study |
| Psychological flexibility (AAQ-II) + fMRI | 2 (Aytur 2021, 2025 study) | Correlational |
| **Psychological flexibility + sMRI** | **0** | **Complete gap** |
| **AAQ-II + brain structure** | **0** | **Complete gap** |

---

## Conclusion

The fMRI literature on psychological flexibility/ACT is **sparse but consistent**:
- Only 4 studies identified
- All use fMRI (functional), none use sMRI (structural)
- Key regions: insula, ACC, PCC, prefrontal cortex, DMN
- Small sample sizes (N = 9 to 42)
- **Zero studies combine psychological flexibility measures with structural MRI**

This confirms the gap: the neural basis of psychological flexibility has been examined functionally but never structurally.
